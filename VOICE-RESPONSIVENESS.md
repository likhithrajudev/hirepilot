# Interview responsiveness and clarity

Normal silence detection is now 1.8 seconds (quick 1.1, patient 3). Completion checks begin earlier and have a 900 ms client deadline and 800 ms server deadline. A stalled check cannot block an otherwise eligible pause. Negative semantic judgments hold the current pause for a bounded interval rather than repeatedly adding twelve seconds. Explicit phrases such as “I'm done” use a 650 ms silence target. Incomplete sentence endings retain a short extra pause.

Finalized browser transcripts can be submitted without another transcription request. Interim transcripts use server transcription with a 1.8-second fallback deadline when live words are available; without live words, the audio-transcription request has a ten-second deadline. This trades the additional transcription pass for faster responses when the browser already has a final transcript. The interviewer still receives the exact recognized text and its existing context-based correction instructions.

Short finalized replies and recognized soft speech no longer require a long VAD burst before they are eligible. Speech-recognition restarts retain prior segments of the answer. Completion requests and transcription are cancelled/ignored when their turn becomes stale. Echo/noise filtering remains active. These are detection targets, not a guarantee of end-to-end latency: model inference, synthesis, browser recognition and connectivity still affect the time until spoken audio.

Neural MP3 segments are now decoded fully before scheduling, avoiding PCM starvation and discontinuities within words. PCM remains a secondary neural route; the device voice is the disclosed last fallback. Neural attempts have a shared ten-second deadline so voice playback cannot remain busy indefinitely on a failed request. Gain has a little headroom, and character-specific voice prompts now prioritize clear consonants, even volume and full voice without breathiness or gravel. Voice identities, model and pacing remain unchanged. Waiting for neural audio is shown as considering the answer, not as the candidate’s turn. This addresses playback continuity and delivery; subjective audible quality has not been graded on the user's audio device.

The format and voice choices were checked against [OpenAI's speech guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/text-to-speech): PCM is raw 24 kHz signed 16-bit little-endian audio, and Marin/Cedar remain recommended voice options. No claim is made that MP3 intrinsically has greater fidelity than PCM; the change avoids artifacts in this application's incremental PCM playback path.

Coding constraints and execution limits are pinned above the editor, including when the problem pane is hidden. AI instructions explicitly explain the saved input bounds and edge conditions; server-owned coding transitions also state them. Constraints are never invented from hidden tests.

Room warnings now occupy a measured header row. A ResizeObserver positions the workspace below that row rather than letting alerts cover code. Floating coding captions are removed; transcript text remains in its own panel. Editor and transcript containers constrain long content to their own columns.

Verification: 190 backend tests, ten frontend tests, TypeScript and production builds pass. Coverage includes stalled completion requests, short finalized answers, explicit completion phrases, finite thinking holds, preserved scope protections and spoken coding constraints. Live end-to-end latency and audible clarity still require a conversation on the actual microphone/speaker setup; an active interview was not refreshed for verification.
